Bricks design system

Bricks didn’t start as a design system. It started as a reimagining of the existing platform — new layouts, new interactions, and a hard look at whether the old visual language still worked.

The challenge

As we moved toward a new product experience across teams, we needed a shared design language that could keep experiences consistent while giving teams a foundation to build and scale from. The challenge was to build this quickly without compromising design quality or consistency.

My role

I helped establish the new design language and its foundations, and worked on defining what the core component layer should look like. I also created the guidelines that helped the team use AI to build components faster and consistently.

Scaling component creation with AI

Instead of prompting AI from scratch for every component, I created an MD guideline that encoded our design rules and decision making framework.

  1. 01

    Define the rules

    Documented the design language, component principles, do’s and don’ts, properties, states and expected behaviours.

  2. 02

    Guide the AI

    The guideline also defined what AI should clarify when a designer’s prompt was incomplete, instead of making assumptions.

  3. 03

    Build with consistency

    The team used the guideline alongside their prompts to generate the initial core components faster, while staying aligned with the system.

  4. 04

    Design review

    Generated components were reviewed and refined by designers before becoming part of the core system.

The goal wasn’t to automate design. It was to automate the repetitive decisions so designers could focus on the quality of the system.
